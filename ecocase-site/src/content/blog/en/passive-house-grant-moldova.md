**In short:** from 1 December 2026 Moldova’s National Center for Sustainable Energy (CNED) runs a pilot programme of grants of up to €60,000 for families building a new home to the Passive House standard. The pilot covers 22 homes and gives priority to families with several children. Final rules will appear on the CNED website, so preparation should start now.

> The information below comes from press coverage in November 2026. Check the official CNED rules before applying.

## What the programme offers

| Product | For | Grant |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Casa Pasivă | a new house built to the Passive House standard | up to €60,000 |
| Casa nZEB avansat | renovating an existing house to advanced nZEB level | up to 50% of eligible costs, max €25,000 |

The pilot includes 22 homes: 12 new builds and 10 renovations. The grant does not pay for the whole house; the family covers the difference from savings or a loan.

## What the Passive House standard requires

A passive house uses very little heating energy thanks to continuous insulation, high-performance windows, airtightness and heat-recovery ventilation. For the grant, three things matter:

1. **A design by certified specialists**, with an energy model of the house.
2. **Verified works** during construction.
3. **Final certification** of the building.

The standard does not prescribe a material; the measured result is what counts.

## How to prepare

1. Check your land documents and request an urban planning certificate.
1. Set the floor area and the total budget, not just the grant amount.
1. Choose a design that can be modelled to the Passive House standard.
1. Line up co-financing (savings or a loan).
1. Follow the CNED website for the rules, form and deadline.
